# Deutschland source audit: 18 new source-only records

Audited 20 August 2026 against the linked publisher or author pages. This covers the 18 files added in commit `7119945` that contained links and descriptions rather than a locally hosted source text. It excludes the complete 2021 Hackethal transcript.

This is a source-and-permissions audit, not legal advice. “No permission” below means that the item page and the publisher’s first-party rights information provide no blanket licence to republish the complete work. It does not rule out quotation permitted by applicable law or permission obtained directly from a rightsholder.

## Decisive result

- Source language: **15 English, 3 German**.
- Official English text: **15**, all because English is the source language. No official English edition was located for the three German *Der Spiegel* interviews.
- Official complete source text or transcript: **17 confirmed** — 13 openly readable and four metered, registration-gated, or paywalled. New Scientist’s official page exists, but its full-text access could not be independently verified because it blocks automated access.
- Explicit blanket licence to republish the complete work locally: **0 of 18**.
- Consequently, none of these 18 should be copied wholesale into Deutschland, and a new complete English translation of a German interview should not be published without translation and republication permission.

The safe way to make these records meaningfully searchable now is to add original English catalogue material: a substantial abstract, topic and claim index, people and works mentioned, and — for interviews — a non-substitutive map of the questions discussed. Add only brief, attributed quotations where justified. Translate this new catalogue material, not the protected source text. Host a full source or translation only after obtaining written permission.

## Recommended implementation order

1. **Make all 18 records useful without mirroring protected text.** Add original English abstracts, concepts, names, works, searchable aliases, and source-access labels (`open`, `metered`, `registration`, `subscription`).
2. **Use existing official English rather than manufacturing a duplicate.** For the 15 English sources, link to the original. For Project Syndicate, also link to its official German edition.
3. **Treat translation as a rights question.** For the three Spiegel interviews, translate only locally authored catalogue prose until Spiegel grants translation and republication rights.
4. **Respect publisher-specific AI restrictions.** Do not process the full Aeon or Economist pieces with AI unless their permissions teams clear that use.
5. **Pursue permissions where a full searchable corpus matters most.** The clearest routes are Spiegel syndication, Aeon syndication, Project Syndicate syndication, Nature RightsLink, Economist licensing, and CBC Reuse & Permission. Record the written licence alongside each local source before adding the complete text.
6. **Index licensed full text separately from catalogue prose.** Store provenance, source language, translator, licence, permission date, and a canonical source URL so search results can distinguish original text, official translation, local translation, and editorial summary.
