## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim transcript.*

Kristin Lynn Sainani asks Deutsch why beauty can be objective. His flower example starts with an evolutionary mismatch: flowers evolved to attract insects, yet people also find them beautiful. Insect preference alone cannot explain the human response, so Deutsch takes the overlap as evidence that some aesthetic truths are independent of any one species, culture, or fashion.

Human judgment still mixes objective, cultural, and genetic standards. Body weight in historical paintings varies culturally; attraction to symmetry may reflect health-related adaptations. Because all three sources feel like beauty from the inside, people cannot yet separate them reliably.

Deutsch compares beauty in art with elegance in science. Elegance can guide conjecture but cannot certify a theory. An ugly fact may refute a beautiful theory, after which a deeper explanation can reveal greater beauty. He extends the same fallibilist pattern to moral progress, mate choice, Mozart, Beethoven, film, and improving aesthetic standards themselves. Objective beauty can be approached through conjecture and criticism without becoming a fixed formula.
