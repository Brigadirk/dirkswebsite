## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim reproduction.*

Deutsch proposes a law of optimism: every interesting problem can be solved. He adds that a problem which is insoluble in principle would also be boring in principle. Whether a problem is interesting may look subjective in the short run, but he expects the distinction between interesting and boring problems to have an objective answer over time.

The law then applies to itself. Why should interesting problems be soluble? Deutsch treats that question as another interesting problem and therefore as one that can itself be solved. The short response anticipates his later distinction between problems blocked by present ignorance and alleged limits built into reality.
