## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim reproduction.*

Deutsch argues that many fields have stopped asking what produces an observed result. Predictions and correlations can be accurate without explaining what produced them. When researchers forget that their explanatory framework was an assumption, they can mistake that framework for something forced on them by the data.

His first example is Bryce DeWitt's objection that he could not feel himself splitting into multiple universes. Deutsch compares this with the old objection that Earth cannot be moving because it feels stationary. Both observations fit more than one explanation. The observation alone cannot choose between them.

He applies the same criticism to behaviourism and genetic studies. Even an extremely strong correlation between a gene and a behaviour would not establish that the gene causes the behaviour. Culture, upbringing, free choice, or another mechanism could produce the same pattern. The missing question is not whether the regularity exists, but why it exists.
