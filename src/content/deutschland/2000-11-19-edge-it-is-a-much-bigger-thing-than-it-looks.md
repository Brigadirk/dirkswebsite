## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim transcript.*

Deutsch treats quantum computation as fundamental physics. Faster machines are only one consequence. Classical Turing computation is, on this view, an approximation to the quantum theory of computation. He sketches an early version of quantum constructor theory, which would classify the physical transformations that can and cannot be performed and the resources each transformation requires.

The structure of quantum computation points to a multiverse. Superposed calculations interfere to produce one result, and Grover's search algorithm makes the hidden computational work especially clear. Deutsch argues that entanglement does not require faster-than-light influence. Quantum information travels locally even when some of it is inaccessible to direct observation.

The interview also covers qubits, quantum cryptography, ion traps, quantum dots, superconducting loops, the limits of classical hardware, and the practical difficulty of building useful quantum computers.
