## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes on the radio segment, not a verbatim transcript.*

CBC's *The Current* asks whether human knowledge has an ultimate boundary. The discussion brings together physicist David Deutsch, astronomer Martin Rees, and philosopher Jennifer Nagel.

Rees argues that human bodies, lifespans, and brains may leave some parts of reality permanently beyond human understanding. Nagel examines the epistemological problem of recognizing a limit from inside the limited system. Deutsch rejects the move from present biological constraints to a fundamental limit on interesting knowledge. People extend their reach through instruments, computers, institutions, explanations, and future forms of intelligence.

The disagreement turns on what counts as a human limit. A person cannot observe every place directly or calculate every result unaided. Deutsch treats these as problems for knowledge and technology, not proof that no explanation can exist. The segment covers cognitive limits, artificial intelligence, scientific instruments, unknowability, infinity, explanation, and the difference between a problem not yet solved and a problem insoluble in principle.
