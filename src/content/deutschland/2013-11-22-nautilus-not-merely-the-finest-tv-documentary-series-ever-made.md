## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim reproduction.*

Deutsch reads Jacob Bronowski's *The Ascent of Man* as an argument about science, art, morality, and political progress. Both science and art reveal hidden structure rather than copy appearances. Both require creativity, criticism, and institutions that permit error correction.

Bronowski's title answers anti-humanism. Humans do more than alter an environment as ants alter one by building a nest. People invent new purposes, explanations, technologies, and ways of life. Their biological "survival kit" is general rather than tied to one habitat. Cultural knowledge can change without waiting for genes to change, allowing ideas to die instead of people.

The article contrasts cultures that permit progress with cultures organized around stasis and obedience. It rejects cultural relativism, inherited-instinct explanations of war and politics, and the claim that human progress is illusory. Deutsch connects Bronowski with Karl Popper, open societies, the Enlightenment, scientific discovery, objective values, and the possibility of continued ascent.
