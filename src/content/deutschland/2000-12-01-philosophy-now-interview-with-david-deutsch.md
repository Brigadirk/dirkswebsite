## Searchable English notes

*This page contains English notes, not a verbatim transcript.*

Filiz Peach asks Deutsch about quantum gravity, the conflict between general relativity and quantum theory, and the possibility of a deeper theory joining physics with epistemology, mathematics, and evolution. Deutsch regards quantum theory as the better current path because its open problems are more tractable.

He argues that knowledge creation belongs in a fundamental account of nature. People may spread through the galaxy and alter its physical constitution, so human action cannot be dismissed as cosmically negligible. He names Karl Popper, Jacob Bronowski, William Godwin, Dennis Sciama, John Wheeler, and Bryce DeWitt among his main influences.

Peach also asks about consciousness and qualia. Deutsch expects consciousness to have a physical explanation, but rejects the idea that explaining it means reducing it to neuron-level description. He considers the subjective experience of seeing red unexplained. On the multiverse, he points to interference and quantum computation as evidence that the unseen part of reality is vastly more complex than the visible part.
